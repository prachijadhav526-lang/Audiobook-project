     History Of Kokan:

Historically, the limits of Konkan have been flexible, and it has been known by additional names like "Aparanta" and "Gomanchal", the latter being defined as the coastal area between the Daman Ganga River in the north and the Gangavalli River in the south. The ancient Sapta Konkan was a larger geographical area that extended from Gujarat to Kerala and included the whole region of coastal Maharashtra and coastal Karnataka Tulunad.However, this segment overlaps the Konkan, Tulunad coast and Malabar coast continuum; and usually corresponds to the southernmost and northernmost stretches of these locales respectively

The Konkan is a stretch of land by the western coast of India, bound by the river Daman Ganga at Damaon in the north, to Anjediva Island next to Karwar town in the south; with the Arabian Sea to the west and the Deccan plateau to the east.The hinterland east of the coast has numerous river valleys, riverine islands and the hilly slopes known as the Western Ghats; that lead up into the tablelands of the Deccan. The Konkan region has been recognised by name, since at least the time of Strabo, in the third century CE.It had a thriving mercantile port with Arab tradesmen from the 10th century onwards.The best-known islands of Konkan are Ilhas de Goa, the site of the Goa state's capital at Panjim; also, the Seven Islands of Bombay, on which lies Mumbai, the capital of Maharashtra & the headquarters of Konkan Division.

   Two Legend men have revolutionized the history of Kokan

Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar (The Father of Indian Constitution)

His family was of Marathi background from the town of Ambadawe (Mandangad taluka) in Ratnagiri district of Koakn Maharashtra. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar was an Indian jurist, economist, social reformer and statesman who chaired the committee that drafted the Constitution of India based on the debates of the Constituent Assembly of India n 1990, the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award, was posthumously conferred on Ambedkar. The salutation Jai Bhim used by followers honours him. He is also referred to by the honorific Babasaheb, meaning "Respected Father". Ambedkar had openly shared his concern in the Constituent Assembly that country might lose both its Constitution and its democratic freedoms in the future. He believed that the lack of a strong democratic tradition, the widespread presence of ‘bhakti’ or political ‘hero worship’ and the large gap between the republican promises of the Constitution.

Chattrapati Shivaji Maharaj (The Founder of Maratha Kigndom Of India)

The Rajyabhishek (coronation) of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, held on June 6, 1674, at Raigad Fort, marks a monumental chapter in Indian history. It was not merely a royal ceremony, but a bold political and cultural declaration of Swarajya—self-rule. Shivaji Maharaj’s coronation re-established the Hindu sovereignty that had faded under centuries of foreign domination. This grand event symbolized the birth of the Maratha Empire, and it remains a source of inspiration, pride, and cultural revival for Indians across generations.Born in 1630 at Shivneri Fort, Shivaji Bhosale rose from a jagirdar’s son to a formidable warrior and leader. His guerrilla warfare tactics, strategic forts, and astute diplomacy enabled him to carve out a kingdom amid the declining power of the Mughal Empire and the Bijapur Sultanate. Before the Rajyabhishek, Shivaji was perceived by some as a rebellious jagirdar or regional warlord. Though respected by his followers, the lack of formal royal legitimacy limited his authority in the eyes of many.
